# spouse-02 Authoring Notes

## Case Core

- Case: `spouse-02`
- Surface bait:
  - Shared-printer output makes party A look like the aggressor who tried to exclude party B from the school contact network.
- Anchor truth:
  - The leaked postpartum counseling termination record originated from party B scanning and forwarding it through an auxiliary email path.
- Core dilemma:
  - Party B reframes disclosure as child protection.
  - Party A crossed a boundary by opening party B's mail and drafting a contact exclusion notice.

## Parties

- Party A: `나희주`
  - Fear axis: counseling history becoming a parenting stigma
  - Verbal tell axis: self-correction, legalistic reframing, breath-counting under shame
  - Judge-facing partner reference: `제 남편`

- Party B: `강도윤`
  - Fear axis: being seen as the father who leaked his wife's mental-health record
  - Verbal tell axis: sacrifice stacking, victim preload, moral reframing
  - Judge-facing partner reference: `제 아내`

## High-Risk Disputes

- `d-1` party B leaked the counseling termination record to school staff.
  - This is the moral center of the case.
  - S0-S1 must not expose the direct leak too early.

- `d-2` party A opened mail / printer traces and drafted contact exclusion.
  - Do not over-excuse the access itself.
  - Preserve the line between `drafted` and `sent`.

- `d-3` anonymous parent-chat warning post source.
  - The trap is that it looks external, but the hash/scan path ties back to party B.
  - Keep identity disclosure throttled until evidence pressure rises.

- `d-4` mutual breach of the promise not to weaponize past records.
  - Both parties crossed the line, but in different forms.
  - This dispute should sound morally layered, not symmetrical-by-default.

- `d-5` admissions-file hold.
  - Truth is administrative error, not a report-triggered hold.
  - This is the misconception axis and should not be colored by the emotional disputes too early.

## Dossier Structure

- `dossier-1`:
  - Evidence: `e-1`, `e-2`
  - Disputes: `d-2`, `d-4`

- `dossier-2`:
  - Evidence: `e-3`, `e-4`
  - Disputes: `d-1`, `d-3`, `d-4`
  - Theme: header path and anonymous warning post

- `dossier-3`:
  - Evidence: `e-5`, `e-6`
  - Disputes: `d-1`, `d-3`, `d-5`
  - Theme: scan log and admissions hold memo

## Authoring Order

1. `interrogation`
   - Start with `d-1`, `d-2`, `d-3`
   - Then write `d-4`
   - Finish with `d-5`

2. `evidence_present`
   - Write by evidence sequence `e-1` to `e-6`
   - Keep `early / mid / late` disclosure bands sharply separated

3. `dossier`
   - Write per dossier card, not per dispute
   - Each answer must feel like a direct response to the exact prompt, not a recycled dispute line

## Channel Rules

- `interrogation`
  - Keep S0-S1 from revealing the real leak path, named channel flow, or direct intent too early.
  - `d-1` and `d-3` need especially careful truth throttling.
  - Party B must sound self-justifying in a “protection” register before collapse.
  - Party A must distinguish `봤다 / 확인했다 / 발송하지 않았다` carefully.

- `evidence_present`
  - `e-1/e-2` should pivot around access trace, draft timing, and non-sent status.
  - `e-3/e-4/e-5` should gradually expose source-path identity.
  - `e-6` should cool the case down into an administrative-error axis.

- `dossier`
  - Respect target party in the question id itself.
  - `a|dossier-*.a.*` should sound like party A answering under pressure.
  - `b|dossier-*.b.*` should sound like party B answering under pressure.

## Quality Guardrails

- One sentence, one listener.
- Judge-facing channels must stay in formal endings.
- Preserve honorific/reference consistency:
  - Party A to judge: `제 남편`
  - Party B to judge: `제 아내`
- Do not let party B's “good father” frame erase the record leak.
- Do not let party A's fear frame erase the mail-access boundary crossing.
